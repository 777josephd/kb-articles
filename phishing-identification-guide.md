# How to Identify a Phishing Email

**Audience:** All staff  
**Last updated:** 2026  
**Maintained by:** IT Help Desk

---

## What is phishing?

Phishing is when someone sends you a fake email designed to look like it came from a trustworthy source — your bank, Microsoft, a colleague, or even IT. The goal is to get you to click a link, open an attachment, or hand over your login credentials.

Phishing is the most common way attackers get into organizational systems. You do not need to do anything complicated to protect yourself — you just need to slow down and check before you click.

---

## Warning Signs to Look For

No single sign confirms a phishing email on its own, but the more of these you see, the more suspicious you should be.

### The sender's email address does not match the organization it claims to be from

The display name can say anything — "Microsoft Support" or "IT Help Desk" — but the actual email address behind it tells the truth. Click or hover on the sender's name to reveal the full address. An email claiming to be from Microsoft should come from a microsoft.com address. If it comes from something like `support@micros0ft-helpdesk.net`, it is fake.

### The email creates urgency or pressure

Phrases like "your account will be suspended in 24 hours," "immediate action required," or "respond before end of day" are designed to make you act before you think. Legitimate IT and security teams do not threaten immediate consequences over email without prior notice.

### The link goes somewhere unexpected

Before clicking any link, hover your mouse over it (do not click) and look at the address that appears at the bottom of your screen. If the email claims to be from your organization but the link points to a completely different website, do not click it. A link that looks like `www.company-portal.com.malicious-site.net` is going to `malicious-site.net`, not your company.

### The email asks for your password, MFA code, or personal information

No legitimate system, IT department, or software vendor will ever ask for your password by email. MFA codes should never be shared with anyone. If an email asks for either of these, it is an attack.

### Poor grammar, odd phrasing, or unusual formatting

Many phishing emails contain subtle language errors, awkward sentence structure, or formatting that does not match what you normally receive from that sender. This is not always obvious — modern phishing emails can be well-written — but it is still worth noticing.

### You were not expecting the email

An invoice from a vendor you have never worked with, a package notification for something you did not order, or a password reset email you did not request — these are all worth pausing on before interacting with.

## What to Do If You Receive a Suspicious Email

1. **Do not click any links or open any attachments**
2. **Do not reply to the email** — even a reply confirms your address is active
3. **Report it to the Help Desk** using the contact information at the bottom of this article
4. **If you are using Outlook**, you can use the built-in "Report Phishing" button if it is available in your toolbar
5. **Delete the email** after reporting it

If you are unsure whether an email is legitimate, contact the Help Desk and forward it to us. We would rather check ten harmless emails than have one successful attack go unreported.

---

## What to Do If You Already Clicked a Link or Entered Your Credentials

**Contact the Help Desk immediately.** Do not wait to see if anything happens. Time matters — the faster we know, the faster we can lock down your account and limit any damage.

Tell us:
- What you clicked or entered
- When it happened
- What website or page you were taken to, if you remember

You will not be in trouble for reporting it. Reporting quickly is the right thing to do.

---

## When to Contact the Help Desk

- You received an email that seems suspicious and want a second opinion
- You clicked a link or attachment before realizing the email may be fake
- You entered your password or MFA code on a site that may not have been legitimate
- You received a password reset email or MFA prompt you did not request

---

## What to have ready when you contact us

- The **sender's email address** (the full address, not just the display name)
- The **subject line** of the email
- A **screenshot or forwarded copy** of the email if possible
- Whether you **clicked anything or entered any information**
- Approximately **when** you received or interacted with the email
